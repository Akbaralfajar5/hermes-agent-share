# English Learning Roadmap: Basic → Fluent (1 Hour/Week)

## Your Profile
- **Current Level:** Basic (can read simple sentences, understand basic words, vocabulary gaps)
- **Goal:** Read and speak fluently
- **Time:** 1 hour/week (≈ 4 hours/month, ≈ 48 hours/year)
- **Method:** Combination (video + reading + writing + conversation)

---

## Strategy Overview

With only **1 hour/week**, you MUST be ruthlessly efficient. No fluff, no wasted time.

**Core principle:** Focus on **high-frequency words first** (80/20 rule). The 1,000 most common English words cover ~80% of everyday conversation and reading.

---

## Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1-12 | 12 hours total)

### Goal
Master 500-1000 high-frequency words + basic grammar patterns.

### Weekly Routine (1 hour)
- **15 min:** Vocabulary drilling (Anki flashcards)
- **20 min:** Video lesson (grammar + context)
- **15 min:** Reading practice (graded readers)
- **10 min:** Speaking practice (record yourself)

### Resources

**Vocabulary (Anki Decks)**
- "English 1000 Most Common Words" (Anki)
- Focus: nouns, verbs, adjectives, prepositions
- Review: 10-15 new cards/day (takes ~5 min)

**Video Lessons (15-20 min/week)**
- English Addict with Mr. Duncan (YouTube) — slow, clear, beginner-friendly
- Pick ONE video per week, watch 2-3x (first time: listen, second: read subtitles, third: no subtitles)
- Topics: present tense, common verbs, daily routines

**Reading (Graded Readers)**
- Penguin Readers Level 1 (A1-A2)
- Oxford Bookworms Starter
- 1-2 short stories per month (10-15 pages each)
- Read with dictionary nearby, don't translate everything

**Speaking Practice**
- Use Google Translate's voice feature or Forvo to hear pronunciation
- Record yourself reading a paragraph (1-2 min)
- Listen back, compare with native speaker

### Milestones
- Week 4: Know 250 high-frequency words
- Week 8: Know 500 high-frequency words
- Week 12: Know 1000 high-frequency words + can read simple stories

## Phase 2: Intermediate (Weeks 13-26 | 14 hours total)

### Goal
Build conversational fluency + read longer texts.

### Weekly Routine (1 hour)
- **10 min:** Vocabulary (expand to 1500-2000 words)
- **15 min:** Video lesson (conversational English)
- **20 min:** Reading practice (longer texts, news articles)
- **15 min:** Speaking/writing practice (write short paragraphs, speak)

### Resources

**Vocabulary**
- Anki: "English 2000 Most Common Words"
- Add context sentences (not just isolated words)

**Video Lessons**
- TED-Ed (English subtitles, 5-10 min videos)
- Rachel's English (YouTube) — natural, conversational
- Pick topics you're interested in (tech, food, travel, etc.)

**Reading**
- BBC Learning English (articles + audio)
- Newsela (news adapted for learners)
- Medium articles (tech/personal blogs, simpler writing)
- Start with 300-500 word articles

**Speaking/Writing**
- Write 3-5 sentences daily about your day (journal)
- Use Grammarly to check
- Join r/WriteStreak (Reddit) — post daily, get corrections
- Use HelloTalk app (language exchange, text + voice)

### Milestones
- Week 16: Can read BBC Learning English articles without dictionary
- Week 20: Can write 5-10 sentence paragraphs with minimal errors
- Week 26: Can have basic conversations (text or voice)

## Phase 3: Advanced (Weeks 27+ | ongoing)

### Goal
Near-native fluency in reading + conversational speaking.

### Weekly Routine (1 hour)
- **5 min:** Vocabulary (specialized/advanced words)
- **20 min:** Reading (news, blogs, technical articles)
- **20 min:** Speaking (conversation partner or voice practice)
- **15 min:** Listening (podcasts, YouTube, movies)

### Resources

**Reading**
- Medium (tech/business articles)
- Hacker News (tech news, comments)
- The Guardian (news + analysis)
- Reddit (r/AskReddit, r/explainlikeimfive)

**Speaking**
- Tandem app (language exchange, voice calls)
- ConversationExchange.com (find language partners)
- Italki (affordable tutors, $5-15/hour)
- Join Discord communities (tech, gaming, etc.) and voice chat

**Listening**
- TED Talks (full videos, no subtitles)
- Podcasts: "English Learning for Curious Minds", "The Daily" (NPR)
- YouTube channels: Crash Course, Kurzgesagt (educational, clear English)

**Writing**
- Start a blog (Medium, Dev.to)
- Write technical documentation (if you code)
- Contribute to open-source projects (read + write English)

### Milestones
- Month 9: Can read news articles and understand 90%+
- Month 12: Can have 10-15 min conversations without major pauses
- Month 18: Can watch movies/shows with English subtitles and understand most

## Efficiency Tips (Critical for 1 hour/week)

1. **Spaced Repetition is King**
   - Use Anki for vocabulary (proven to work)
   - Review cards daily (5-10 min) outside your 1 hour/week

2. **Immersion in Small Doses**
   - Change your phone language to English
   - Watch YouTube in English (even if you don't understand everything)
   - Read Reddit comments (natural English, not textbook)

3. **Focus on Comprehensible Input**
   - Don't watch movies you don't understand
   - Watch content slightly above your level (with subtitles)
   - Rewatch videos 2-3x (each time you'll catch more)

4. **Active Practice, Not Passive**
   - Don't just watch videos — pause, repeat, write down new words
   - Don't just read — write summaries
   - Don't just listen — speak back

5. **Track Progress**
   - Keep a vocabulary log (Google Sheets: date, word, example sentence)
   - Record yourself monthly (compare with previous month)
   - Read the same article after 3 months (you'll notice improvement)

## Grammar Quiz Assistance Style

When user sends grammar/sentence exercises (screenshots or text):

**Response format:**
- **CORRECT** ✅ or **INCORRECT** ❌ — one line max
- Brief reason only if needed (1 sentence, Indonesian)
- No long explanations unless user asks
- User explicitly said: "tolong jawab correct incorrect ya" — direct answers only

**Subject-Verb Agreement Checklist (common quiz patterns):**
1. Missing subject → INCORRECT (e.g. "During the meeting discussed..." — who discussed?)
2. Missing verb → INCORRECT (e.g. "The tall trees along the road." — no predicate)
3. Missing auxiliary verb → INCORRECT (e.g. "The plane circling..." → needs "is")
4. Prepositional phrases don't affect verb agreement (ignore "in the box", "on the table", etc.)
5. Singular subject + singular verb → CORRECT (e.g. "The name...is")
6. Plural subject + plural verb → CORRECT (e.g. "The dishes...need")
7. Passive voice counts as verb (e.g. "are watered and fed" = valid verb phrase)

**Language:** Answer in Indonesian, casual lo/gue style. Keep it short.

**Listening/Reading Comprehension (Restatements, TOEFL-style):**
Unlike grammar quizzes, comprehension questions want DETAILED explanations:
- Explain in Indonesian, break down each answer choice
- Use side-by-side synonym vs similar-sound comparison tables
- Show which words map to which (sinonim pairs) with `| Original | Restatement |` tables
- Highlight distractor traps (similar sounds that are NOT synonyms)
- Work through the full example: dialogue → meaning → why correct answer matches → why others are wrong
- User sends Google Forms with audio — may not be accessible; ask for screenshots if form fails
- **Reading Comprehension (TOEFL Reading Passages):**
- User sends photos/scans of reading passages with multiple-choice questions
- Answer in Indonesian, explain all options (why right + why wrong)
- Question types: topic, main idea, best title, subject of paragraph, **stated detail (Skill 3)**
- Detailed techniques + elimination strategy: see `references/toefl-reading-comprehension.md`
- Stated detail: jawaban ada di passage (paraphrase), urutan berurutan, hindari trap (wrong timing, opposite, keyword bait)
- Detailed techniques + worked examples:
  - Restatements (Skill 1): see `references/toefl-listening-restatements.md`
  - Negatives (Skill 2): see `references/toefl-listening-negatives.md`

## Listening Strategies (While vs Then)

User asked about two approaches:
- **Listening while reading** = dengerin sambil baca barengan (simultaneous) — bagus buat build koneksi audio→text, improve pronunciation awareness
- **Listening then reading** = dengerin dulu, baru baca (sequential) — bagus buat test comprehension murni, latihan tanpa text support

**For TOEFL listening prep:** start with "listening while reading" (easier), graduate to "listening then reading" (harder, more realistic for test conditions).

## Pitfalls to Avoid

❌ **Don't translate everything** — you'll get stuck in translation mode, not thinking in English
❌ **Don't memorize grammar rules** — learn through context and examples
❌ **Don't watch passive content** — pause, repeat, write, speak
❌ **Don't skip speaking** — reading alone won't make you fluent
❌ **Don't expect fast results** — 1 hour/week = slow but steady progress (1-2 years to fluency)
❌ **Don't switch methods constantly** — stick with one approach for 4-8 weeks before changing
